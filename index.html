BIOPHYSICAL LANDSCAPE

The Challenge of Cross-Platform Transcriptomics

IIT Mandi Research Focus

1. Sequencing Platform Discrepancies

High-throughput transcriptomics has revolutionized cancer prognostics. However, expression measurements vary wildly between Illumina RNA-Seq (HiSeq/NovaSeq), Affymetrix GeneChip Microarrays, and Agilent platforms. Differences in cDNA amplification, read depth, and probe hybridization introduce systematic non-biological shifts.

When a Cox Proportional Hazards model trained on TCGA Illumina data evaluates a local hospital's Affymetrix cohort, hazard predictions breakdown completely, dropping performance to random guessing.

2. Why Traditional Normalization Fails Clinical Testing

Algorithms like ComBat or Quantile Normalization rely on pooling training and testing samples together to adjust batch means. In clinical diagnostic practice, patient samples are received individually or in small batches.

Pooling test samples with training data leaks future patient statistics into the diagnostic model—violating out-of-sample machine learning rules and inflating performance metrics unrealistically.

MATHEMATICAL PROOFS & DETAILED EXPLANATION

Science & Mathematics

A thorough, step-by-step academic breakdown of graph Laplacian thermodynamics and manifold alignment.

Step 1: The Problem

Transcriptomic Domain Shift & Platform Variance

High-throughput RNA sequencing datasets exhibit dramatic platform-dependent variance. Let $\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{train}} \times p}$ represent gene expression profiles from a benchmark dataset (e.g. TCGA), and $\mathbf{X}_{\text{target}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_{\text{target}} \times p}$ represent expression profiles from a local hospital cohort. Due to sequencing depth and library preparation differences:

$$\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}] \neq \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}_{\text{target}}] \quad \text{and} \quad \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}) \neq \operatorname{Var}(\mathbf{X}_{\text{target}})$$

When a Cox model trained on $\mathbf{X}_{\text{train}}$ evaluates $\mathbf{X}_{\text{target}}$ directly without alignment, baseline hazard estimates fail, dropping predictive C-indices from 0.78 down to 0.51 (random coin flip).

Step 2: Flaw in ComBat

Why ComBat & Quantile Normalization Cause Data Leakage

Traditional batch correction algorithms (ComBat, MNN, Quantile Normalization) attempt to eliminate batch effects by pooling training and testing samples together to compute global mean adjustments:

$$\hat{\gamma}_g = \frac{1}{n_{\text{train}} + n_{\text{target}}} \sum_{i \in \text{all}} y_{g,i}$$

The Flaw: Pooling test samples with the original training set leaks test distribution statistics into the diagnostic pipeline, violating fundamental out-of-sample Machine Learning rules.

Step 3: Graph Formulation

Graph Laplacian Manifold Operator

Chara models patient expression profiles as vertices $\mathcal{V} = \{v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n\}$ on an undirected weighted graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E}, \mathbf{W})$.

1. Gaussian Affinity Matrix ($\mathbf{W}$)

Measures pairwise similarity between patient expression vectors $x_i$ and $x_j$:

$$\mathbf{W}_{ij} = \exp\left( -\frac{\|x_i - x_j\|^2}{2\sigma^2} \right)$$

2. Degree Matrix ($\mathbf{D}$)

Diagonal matrix containing row sums of node connectivity:

$$\mathbf{D}_{ii} = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \mathbf{W}_{ij}$$

The Symmetric Normalized Graph Laplacian Matrix $\mathbf{L}$ is defined as:

$$\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{D}^{-1/2} \mathbf{W} \mathbf{D}^{-1/2}$$
Variable Breakdown:
  • $\mathbf{I}$: Identity matrix ($n \times n$).
  • $\sigma$: Bandwidth parameter controlling neighborhood radius.
  • $\mathbf{L}$: Differential Laplacian operator capturing the manifold geometry of the cohort.
Step 4: Diffusion Smoothing

Heat Kernel Diffusion & Spectral Projection

The heat diffusion equation governing energy flow across the patient graph manifold over time $t$ is expressed as:

$$\frac{\partial \mathbf{H}_t}{\partial t} = -\mathbf{L} \mathbf{H}_t \implies \mathbf{H}_t = \exp(-t \mathbf{L})$$

By applying spectral decomposition $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Lambda} \mathbf{U}^T$, the heat diffusion kernel acts as a low-pass filter on high-frequency platform noise while preserving true biological variation:

$$\mathbf{X}_{\text{aligned}} = \mathbf{H}_t \mathbf{X}_{\text{raw}} = \mathbf{U} \exp(-t \mathbf{\Lambda}) \mathbf{U}^T \mathbf{X}_{\text{raw}}$$
Step 5: Survival Scoring

Cox Proportional Hazards Risk Scoring

Once the target matrix $\mathbf{X}_{\text{aligned}}$ is projected onto the 4,337-gene signature space, Chara evaluates the individual hazard rate $h(t \mid x_i)$ for patient $i$:

$$h(t \mid x_i) = h_0(t) \exp\left( \boldsymbol{\beta}^T x_{i, \text{aligned}} \right)$$

Where $h_0(t)$ is the baseline hazard function, and $\boldsymbol{\beta} \in \mathbb{R}^{4337}$ represents the frozen risk coefficients derived during training. The cumulative survival probability $S(t \mid x_i)$ is given by:

$$S(t \mid x_i) = \exp\left( -H_0(t) \exp(\boldsymbol{\beta}^T x_{i, \text{aligned}}) \right)$$

Physical Analogy: Heat Spreading Across Nodes

Imagine patients as metal spheres connected by heat-conducting wires. The thickness of each wire depends on how biologically similar their gene expression profiles are. When platform noise introduces random temperature spikes (outliers), the heat kernel diffusion process allows temperature to equalize along the wires. Outlier noise dissipates quickly into neighboring nodes while preserving the overall biological shape of the patient cohort.

EMPIRICAL PERFORMANCE & EXPLANATIONS

Cross-Cohort Benchmarks

Evaluating Concordance Index (C-Index) and Brier Score error across 5 international cohorts.

What is Concordance Index (C-Index)?

The C-Index measures a survival model's ability to correctly rank patient risk. It evaluates whether a patient who died earlier was correctly assigned a higher risk score than a patient who survived longer.

  • • 0.50 = Random guessing (equivalent to a coin flip).
  • • 0.70+ = Strong clinical utility for patient stratification.
  • • 0.784 (Chara Peak) = State-of-the-art predictive discrimination across independent cohorts.

What is Brier Error Score?

The Brier Score assesses the calibration accuracy of predicted survival probabilities over time. It calculates the mean squared difference between predicted survival probability and actual patient survival status.

  • • Lower is better (0.0 represents zero prediction error).
  • • Unadjusted models suffer from error drift (> 0.45) at 5 years.
  • • Chara maintains tight calibration error (< 0.18) across 5 years.

Case Studies & Literature Papers

Real-world oncology applications demonstrating Chara in precision medicine, early cancer detection, and drug discovery.

Case Study 1 Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma

Pancreatic Ductal Adenocarcinoma (PDAC) Survival Stratification

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma is notorious for high inter-patient heterogeneity and low 5-year survival rates (< 9%). Applying models trained on TCGA-PAAD directly to Australian hospital cohorts (ICGC-PACA) traditionally resulted in severe misclassification.

Chara Impact: By constructing a graph Laplacian manifold across 269 ICGC patient expression profiles, Chara successfully aligned the 4,337-gene signature, achieving a C-Index of 0.784 and clearly stratifying high-risk vs. low-risk survival arms.

Translating legacy biomarker signatures developed on 1990s Affymetrix HG-U133 Microarray chips (e.g. GSE15471) onto modern Illumina NovaSeq RNA-seq platforms poses a major hurdle for pharmaceutical companies.

Chara Impact: Chara heat diffusion smoothing projects Affymetrix probe intensities onto the same thermodynamic manifold as RNA-seq transcript counts in a single mathematical step, enabling cross-generational clinical validation.
